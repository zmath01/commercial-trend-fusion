Methodology

The complete, formal description of what this project computes and why. Every formula below has a direct implementation in scripts/. Nothing on this site is a number without a script behind it.

1. Design principles

  1. Reproducibility first. The corpus is fetched by scripts/fetch_arxiv.py, every derived number comes from a script, and the site is generated from those numbers.
  2. Honest baselines. Predictions are compared against controls that share the same structural conditions (same frequency, same two-hop neighborhood) — not against arbitrary "random" sets that make the result look good by construction.
  3. Report failure too. Modest ρ, low AUC, base rates — all published. A tool that only ever shows impressive numbers is not a tool, it is marketing.
  4. No private funnels. Everything is public: code, data pipeline, results. The product is the analysis, not access to it.

3. Phrase mining (concepts as atoms)

Concepts are multi-word academic phrases extracted with the association measure of Mikolov et al. (2013) word2phrase. For adjacent tokens $a, b$ with counts $c(a), c(b), c(ab)$ and total adjacent-pair count $N$, the merge score is

$$\text{score}(a,b) \;=\; \frac{c(ab) - \delta}{c(a)\,c(b)}\,N, \qquad \delta = f_{\min}$$

Merging iterates until no pair passes the threshold or phrases reach $n_{\max}=4$ tokens. Final phrases must appear at least $f_{\min}$ times. Strong unigrams (frequency ≥ $10 f_{\min}$) are added so single-token commercial terms (llm, gpu, qubit, bitcoin) enter the network too. Stopwords (edge-position), pure digits and length-1 tokens are excluded. Implementation: scripts/build_lexicon.py.

4. Concept network

For each year (and for cumulative windows) we build a weighted undirected graph: nodes = phrases with document frequency ≥ $f_{\text{hot}}$; edge $(u,v)$ = number of papers containing both phrases, kept if weight ≥ $e_{\min}$.

Data structure. Adjacency is stored as a sparse CSR matrix (scipy) — $O(V+E)$ memory — rather than nested Python dicts, which is the concrete scaling choice that lets this pipeline run on a laptop. Node id ↔ term maps are CSVs. Implementation: scripts/build_graph.py.

5. Signals

5.1 Growth slope

For a term with per-year counts $f_t$, the growth signal is the slope of $\log(1 + f_t)$ regressed on year — log-linear growth rate with $R^2$ and $p$.

5.2 Hockey-stick (takeoff detector)

$$h \;=\; \frac{\text{slope}(\text{last } w \text{ years})}{\text{slope}(\text{previous } w \text{ years})}$$

$h > 1$: accelerating; $h < 0$: declining after prior growth. This is the commercial-takeoff pattern: slow research accumulation followed by a sudden increase in activity (LLMs, superconductors, Bitcoin, …).

5.3 PageRank

Power iteration on the row-stochastic adjacency matrix, $\alpha = 0.85$. Captures "hub" importance in the concept network.

5.4 Effective conductance G(A,B) — the fusion signal

Treat the co-occurrence graph as a resistor network. The resistance distance (Klein & Randić 1993) between nodes $i,j$ is

$$R_{\text{eff}}(i,j) \;=\; L^{+}_{ii} + L^{+}_{jj} - 2 L^{+}_{ij}, \qquad G(i,j) \;=\; \frac{1}{R_{\text{eff}}(i,j)}$$

where $L = D - A$ is the graph Laplacian and $L^{+}$ its Moore–Penrose inverse. Rather than forming $L^{+}$ (dense, $O(V^3)$), we solve the equivalent sparse system $L x = e_i - e_j$ with conjugate gradients (Jacobi preconditioner) and read $R_{\text{eff}} = x_i - x_j$. Complexity is roughly $O(E \sqrt{\kappa})$ per pair, which makes full-network conductance practical on a laptop for thousands of pairs. Disconnected pairs have $G = 0$.

Why G ≠ co-occurrence: for pairs with zero direct co-occurrence, G remains well-defined via all indirect paths. It is the mathematically principled version of "how close are these two concepts in the whole network" — no arbitrary subgraph truncation, no embeddings required.

6. Prediction tasks

  • Task A (concept growth): will a term's frequency grow in the next window? Signals: history-window slope and hockey-stick ratio.
  • Task B (pair fusion): will two never-co-occurring concepts first co-occur? Candidates: 2-hop non-edges (A–w–B with strength $I = \min(w_{Aw}, w_{wB})$), ranked by full-network G. This is the "cross-field fusion early-warning" watchlist.

7. Evaluation (the part most similar projects skip)

7.1 Task A

Outcome = future-window log-slope. Metrics: Spearman ρ with permutation p-value (1000 shuffles), bootstrap 95% CI (1000 resamples), ROC AUC of above-median future growth, and lift@K: mean outcome of the top-K vs a random baseline matched on 5 frequency bands (so we are not just re-discovering "popular terms stay popular").

7.2 Task B

Hit = first co-occurrence in the test window with ≥3 joint papers. Reported rates: pool base rate (all candidates), top-100 by G, top-100 by I (cheap 2-hop strength), naive control (random pairs matched on frequency bands only), and structural control (same A, same bridge w, random alternative B′ — matched two-hop structure). The structural control is the fair test: it isolates whether G adds signal beyond the neighborhood structure. We also report AUC of G and of I on the candidate pool, and a permutation p for the top-100-vs-structural-control lift.

Why this matters: a control that omits the structural condition (e.g. random pairs without a two-hop path) is guaranteed to under-hit — nearby pairs link more often than far pairs by construction. Comparing against it inflates every result. We do not do that.

8. Known limitations

  • Capped sampling: cross-category comparisons need the full fetch.
  • arXiv ≠ market: funding, patents, hiring and price data are documented extensions (docs/data_sources.md), not yet integrated.
  • Multiple testing: p-values are per-test. Use the whole picture, not one number.
  • Phrase mining is lexicon-based: brand-new phrasings lag by design.

9. Anti-patterns this project explicitly rejects

  • Unreproducible headline metrics — a number in a README with no script behind it. Every number here is generated.
  • Misnamed methods — "random walk" for deterministic enumeration, "semantic search" for lexical overlap. Names mean what they say.
  • Unmatched controls — controls must share the structural conditions of the treatment arm, or the comparison is meaningless.
  • Inconsistent baselines — one experiment, one baseline definition, reported every time.
  • Fancy names for standard heuristics — the physics vocabulary here (effective conductance) is backed by an actual Laplacian solve, and the code says so.
